# Equipment: Wall Ball

## Description
A wall ball is a large, soft, loosely filled ball designed to be thrown at a wall and caught on the rebound. It is deliberately squashy and oversized so it can be caught safely at speed, which is what separates it from a firmer [medicine ball](./medicine-balls.md). The standard movement pairs a front squat with an overhead throw to a marked target height, making it one of the few genuinely full body exercises that also drives the heart rate hard.

## Equipment Type
Functional

## How To Use
- Stand roughly an arm's length from the wall, holding the ball at chest height with elbows underneath it.
- Squat to at least parallel, keeping the ball close and the trunk upright.
- Drive up out of the squat and use that momentum to throw the ball to the target height.
- Catch it on the way down with soft arms and let it carry you into the next squat.
- Find a rhythm rather than treating each rep separately. Breaking the cycle costs far more energy than it saves.

## Benefits
- Combines a lower body strength pattern with an upper body push in one movement.
- Raises the heart rate quickly, so it doubles as conditioning.
- Trains full depth squatting under fatigue.
- Teaches timing and force transfer from legs to arms.

## Targeted Muscles
- Quadriceps and glutes through the squat and the drive.
- Shoulders and triceps in the throw.
- Trunk in stabilising between the two.
- Calves and hamstrings in the extension.

## Common Exercises
- Standard wall balls for reps, commonly in sets of 20 to 25.
- Unbroken sets to a target, such as 100 reps, as a conditioning benchmark.
- Squat and throw for height rather than reps, for power.
- Chest passes or rotational throws for upper body and trunk work.

## Skill Level
- Approachable for beginners once a squat to depth is comfortable.
- Requires enough shoulder mobility to press overhead without arching the lower back.
- Start with a lighter ball and a lower target rather than the competition standard.

## Safety Tips
- Check the wall will take repeated impact. Not every wall will, and a wall ball can do real damage to plasterboard.
- Keep clear space around you and never stand directly beside someone throwing.
- Catch with bent arms and absorb the ball rather than blocking it.
- Stop when squat depth or the catch starts to break down; fatigue on this movement shows up as a rounded back.

## Alternatives
- [Medicine balls](./medicine-balls.md), workable against a solid wall though firmer and less forgiving to catch.
- [Dumbbell](./dumbbells.md) thrusters where no wall is suitable, giving the same squat to press pattern.
- Barbell thrusters for a heavier version of the same movement.
- Goblet squats paired with an overhead press to train the halves separately.
