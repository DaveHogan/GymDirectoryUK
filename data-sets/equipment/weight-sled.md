# Equipment: Weight Sled

## Description
A weight sled is a low steel frame loaded with plates and driven across the floor, either pushed from upright posts or dragged on a strap or rope. It differs from a [Prowler](./prowler.md) mainly in intent: the sled is designed to be both pushed and pulled, and many are low enough to run behind rather than lean into. Because there is no lowering phase, a sled produces very little muscle soreness for the effort involved, which is why it appears so often in conditioning and rehabilitation alike.

## Equipment Type
Functional

## How To Use
- Check the surface first. Sleds need a floor that will take them, usually turf, rubber or a dedicated track.
- Load conservatively to begin with. A sled that will not move teaches nothing.
- To push, grip the posts, lean in with a straight line from heel to head and drive with short powerful steps.
- To pull, walk backwards with a low hip position, or face away and pull hand over hand on a rope from a seated or standing position.
- Keep the effort continuous over the working distance rather than stopping and restarting.

## Benefits
- Builds leg drive and pushing strength with almost no eccentric loading, so recovery is fast.
- Highly conditioning without the impact of running.
- Loads the whole body in a pattern that transfers directly to sport.
- Easy to scale precisely by adding or removing plates.

## Targeted Muscles
- Quadriceps, glutes and calves, which do the majority of the work in a push.
- Hamstrings and the posterior chain during a backward drag.
- Trunk and shoulders in maintaining the body position against the load.
- Grip and upper back during a rope pull.

## Common Exercises
- Heavy pushes over short distances, typically 10m to 20m, for strength.
- Lighter sprints for power and conditioning.
- Backward drags for the quads and for knee friendly loading.
- Hand over hand rope pulls, drawing the sled towards you from a seated or standing position.

## Skill Level
- Suitable for beginners. There is very little technique to learn compared with a barbell lift.
- The main skill is pacing: most people start far too fast and stall halfway.
- Heavier loads demand a stronger trunk position and are better introduced gradually.

## Safety Tips
- Make sure the lane ahead is clear and long enough before you start.
- Do not use a sled on a surface it can damage or where it will not slide predictably.
- Keep a braced trunk and a neutral spine rather than rounding under the load.
- Take care when stopping a loaded sled, particularly on a slick surface.

## Alternatives
- [Prowler](./prowler.md), essentially the same tool with a higher frame.
- [Leg press machine](./leg-press-machine.md) for the drive pattern where no sled is available.
- [Hack squat](./hack-squat.md) for loaded knee extension under a similar body angle.
- Heavy walking lunges as a conditioning substitute.
