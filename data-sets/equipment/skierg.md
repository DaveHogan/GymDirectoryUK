# Equipment: SkiErg

## Description
The SkiErg is a standing ergometer that reproduces the double-pole action of cross-country skiing. A handle on each end of a cord is pulled down from overhead, driving a flywheel and a damper that sets the resistance. Unlike most cardio machines it is upper body led, drawing heavily on the lats, triceps and trunk while the legs contribute through a hinge rather than a stride. It is a fixture of functional fitness gyms, and 1000m is its usual test piece.

## Equipment Type
Cardio

## How To Use
- Set the damper. Most people are best served somewhere in the middle rather than at the highest setting.
- Stand tall, feet hip width, and reach up to take a handle in each hand with arms nearly straight.
- Drive down by hinging at the hips and pulling the handles past your thighs, letting the legs bend to absorb the movement.
- Finish with the hands below the hips and the trunk folded forward, then stand back up and let the handles return under control.
- Keep the rhythm even. Pulling harder rather than faster is what moves the numbers.

## Benefits
- Builds cardiovascular fitness with far more upper body involvement than a bike or a treadmill.
- Low impact, so it loads the engine without loading the joints.
- Trains the hinge pattern and trunk bracing under fatigue.
- Scales cleanly from a beginner to an elite athlete through effort rather than setup.

## Targeted Muscles
- Latissimus dorsi and the mid back, which do most of the pulling.
- Triceps and shoulders through the drive.
- Abdominals and the deep trunk muscles, working hard to transmit force.
- Glutes and hamstrings through the hip hinge.

## Common Exercises
- Steady intervals, such as 5 x 500m with equal rest, for building the engine.
- A continuous 1000m for time, the standard benchmark distance.
- Short sprints of 100m to 250m for power.
- Alternating with running or rowing in a circuit to train under fatigue.

## Skill Level
- Suitable for beginners. The movement is easy to learn and difficult to do badly enough to hurt yourself.
- Technique rewards practice: most newcomers pull with the arms alone and tire quickly.
- People with shoulder restrictions may need to shorten the overhead reach.

## Safety Tips
- Warm the shoulders and upper back before hard efforts.
- Hinge at the hips rather than rounding through the lower back.
- Keep hold of the handles on the return rather than letting them snap upward.
- Build distance gradually. The upper body fatigues faster than most people expect.

## Alternatives
- [Rowing machine](./rowing-machine.md), the closest substitute, training a similar pull and the same energy system.
- Lat pulldowns or straight arm pulldowns for the pulling pattern without the conditioning.
- [Battle ropes](./battle-ropes.md) for upper body conditioning where no ergometer is available.
